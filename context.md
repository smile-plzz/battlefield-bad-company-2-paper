### Research Scope & Boundaries
- **Primary Game Object:** *Battlefield: Bad Company 2* (2010), developed by EA DICE, published by Electronic Arts. Directed by Patrick Bach; campaign lead designer & writer David Goldfarb; producer Gordon Van Dyke; audio director Stefan Strandberg.
- **Secondary / Comparative Objects:** *Battlefield: Bad Company* (2008) for character origin and tone; *Call of Duty 4: Modern Warfare* (2007) / *Modern Warfare 2* (2009) as genre comparative benchmarks; *Battlefield 3* (2011) as technical and stylistic departure. *Battlefield: Bad Company 2: Vietnam* is treated as supplementary expansion material.
- **Disciplinary Boundaries:** Game Studies (Primary), Media Studies, Military Studies, Political Communication, Narrative Studies, Psychology of Interactive Media, Cultural Studies, Digital Humanities.
- **Citation Format:** APA 7th Edition.
- **Manuscript Target Length:** 7,000–9,000 words (excluding references, tables, and appendices).

### Central Research Question & Inquiries
- **Primary RQ:** How does *Battlefield: Bad Company 2* combine military satire, squad-based storytelling, destructive gameplay, and multiplayer competition to construct an entertaining yet potentially contradictory representation of modern warfare?
- **Sub-Questions:**
  - **RQ1 (Military Representation):** Depiction of modern warfare through narrative, weapons, environments, and geopolitics.
  - **RQ2 (Military Satire):** Utilization of comedy, irreverence, and action-movie tropes to critique/subvert shooter conventions.
  - **RQ3 (Character Development):** Arcs and dynamics of Preston Marlowe, Sgt. Samuel Redford, George Haggard Jr., and Terrence Sweetwater.
  - **RQ4 (Destruction as Gameplay):** Frostbite 1.5 and Destruction 2.0 transforming static environments into dynamic combat zones.
  - **RQ5 (Squad Identity & Brotherhood):** Construction of military camaraderie through dialogue, banter, and shared hazard.
  - **RQ6 (Campaign vs. Multiplayer):** Differing communication of warfare, teamplay, violence, and competitive achievement.
  - **RQ7 (Sound & Immersion):** Role of High Dynamic Range (HDR) audio, physical weapon recordings, acoustic propagation, and haptic synchronization.
  - **RQ8 (Geopolitics & Ideology):** Cold War redux, US-Russia hostilities, scalar intervention in South America, and non-state proxies.
  - **RQ9 (Spectacle of Destruction):** Tension between environmental ruin as tactical utility vs. pleasurable consumer spectacle.
  - **RQ10 (Contradictions of Military Entertainment):** Can a game meaningfully parody or critique military violence while deriving its commercial and interactive appeal from the simulation and aestheticization of that same violence?
